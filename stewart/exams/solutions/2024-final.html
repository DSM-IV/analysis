\(E'\)은 \(u\mapsto-u\)에 대해 대칭이고 \(uv(u^2+v^2+w^2)\)는 \(u\)에 대해 기함수이므로 \(uv\)항의 적분은 \(0\)이다. 나머지는 \(w\)축을 기준으로 한 구면좌표 \(u=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta,\ v=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta,\ w=\rho\cos\phi\) (\(0\le\rho\le1,\ 0\le\theta\le2\pi,\ 0\le\phi\le\frac\pi2\))로 계산한다. \(u^2+v^2=\rho^2\sin^2\phi\)이므로

\[\begin{aligned}I&=\frac18\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1\rho^2\sin^2\phi\cdot\rho^2\cdot\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi\\&=\frac18\cdot\frac17\cdot2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\phi\,d\phi\\&=\frac18\cdot\frac17\cdot2\pi\cdot\frac23=\frac{\pi}{42}.\end{aligned}\]

(\(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\phi\,d\phi=\int_0^1(1-c^2)\,dc=\frac23\), \(c=\cos\phi\).)

답. \(\dfrac{\pi}{42}\).

채점 포인트 · 자주 하는 실수

  • (a) 크래머 공식: \(y\)의 열(둘째 열)을 \((u,v,w)\)로 바꾼 행렬식 \(\div\det M\). \(\det M=-2\)의 부호에 주의한다.
  • (b) 야코비안은 \(\dfrac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}=\dfrac{1}{\partial(u,v,w)/\partial(x,y,z)}\)이고 절댓값 \(\frac12\)을 곱한다(\(2\)를 곱하거나 \(-\frac12\)을 쓰면 틀림).
  • 영역이 위쪽 반구체 \(w\ge0\)가 됨을 쓰고, \(uv\)항이 \(0\)인 대칭 근거와 구면좌표 \(dV=\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi\)를 명시한다.

(a)

Let \(M\) be the coefficient matrix:

\[\begin{aligned}\det M&=\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\1&-1&1\\1&1&2\end{vmatrix}\\&=1(-2-1)-1(2-1)+1(1+1)\\&=-2\neq0,\end{aligned}\]

so the solution is unique. By Cramer's rule, replace the second column of \(M\) by \((u,v,w)\) and divide by \(\det M\):

\[\begin{vmatrix}1&u&1\\1&v&1\\1&w&2\end{vmatrix}=(2v-w)-u(2-1)+(w-v)=v-u,\] \[y=\frac{v-u}{-2}=\frac{u-v}{2}.\]

(Check: \(u-v=(x+y+z)-(x-y+z)=2y\).)

Answer. \(y=\dfrac{u-v}{2}\).

(b)

Change variables by \(u=x+y+z,\ v=x-y+z,\ w=x+y+2z\). The map is linear with \(\dfrac{\partial(u,v,w)}{\partial(x,y,z)}=\det M=-2\neq0\), so it is one-to-one and

\[\left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\right|=\frac1{|-2|}=\frac12.\]

\(E\) maps onto the upper unit half-ball \(E'=\{u^2+v^2+w^2\le1,\ w\ge0\}\) in \(uvw\)-space, and by (a) \(y=\frac{u-v}2\), so

\[\begin{aligned}I&=\iiint_{E'}\frac{(u-v)^2}{4}\,(u^2+v^2+w^2)\cdot\frac12\,dV\\&=\frac18\iiint_{E'}(u^2+v^2-2uv)(u^2+v^2+w^2)\,dV.\end{aligned}\]

\(E'\) is symmetric under \(u\mapsto-u\) and \(uv(u^2+v^2+w^2)\) is odd in \(u\), so the \(uv\) term integrates to \(0\). For the rest use spherical coordinates about the \(w\)-axis, \(u=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta,\ v=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta,\ w=\rho\cos\phi\) (\(0\le\rho\le1,\ 0\le\theta\le2\pi,\ 0\le\phi\le\frac\pi2\)). \[\begin{aligned}I&=\frac18\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1\rho^2\sin^2\phi\cdot\rho^2\cdot\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi\\&=\frac18\cdot\frac17\cdot2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\phi\,d\phi\\&=\frac18\cdot\frac17\cdot2\pi\cdot\frac23=\frac{\pi}{42}.\end{aligned}\]

(\(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\phi\,d\phi=\int_0^1(1-c^2)\,dc=\frac23\) with \(c=\cos\phi\).)

Answer. \(\dfrac{\pi}{42}\).

(a)

By the product rule, \(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F=\nabla(\rho^{-3})\cdot(\mathbf c\times\mathbf r)+\rho^{-3}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf c\times\mathbf r)\). Since \(z-y,\ x-z,\ y-x\) do not involve \(x,\ y,\ z\) respectively, \(\operatorname{div}(\mathbf c\times\mathbf r)=0\); with \(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\rho^{-3}=-3x\rho^{-5}\) and so on, away from the origin

\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{div}\mathbf F&=-3\rho^{-5}\big[x(z-y)+y(x-z)+z(y-x)\big]\\&=-3\rho^{-5}\cdot0=0.\end{aligned}\]

\[E_\varepsilon=\{x^2+y^2\le1,\ 0\le z\le3,\ x^2+y^2+z^2\ge\varepsilon^2\}.\]

\[\begin{aligned}&\iint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S+\iint_{A_\varepsilon}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S\\&\quad+\iint_{H_\varepsilon}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=0.\end{aligned}\]
