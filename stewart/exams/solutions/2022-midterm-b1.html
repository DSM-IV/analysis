(a)

\(y=0\)을 따라 \((x,0)\to(0,0)\)이면

\[\frac{(\sin x)^2}{x^2}=\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\to1.\]

\(y=-x\)를 따라 \((x,-x)\to(0,0)\)이면 \(\sin y=-\sin x\)이므로

\[\frac{(\sin x-\sin x)^2}{2x^2}=0\to0.\]

두 경로에서 극한값이 \(1\neq0\)으로 다르므로 극한은 존재하지 않는다.

답. 극한은 존재하지 않는다.

(b)

분자를 인수분해하면 \(x^3yz+xy^3z-xyz^3=xyz\,(x^2+y^2-z^2)\)이다. \(R=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\)라 하자. \(|x|,|y|,|z|\le R\)이므로 \(|xyz|\le R^3\)이고, \(|x^2+y^2-z^2|\le x^2+y^2+z^2=R^2\)이다. 따라서

\[\left|\frac{xyz\,(x^2+y^2-z^2)}{x^2+y^2+z^2}\right|\le\frac{R^3\cdot R^2}{R^2}=R^3.\]

\((x,y,z)\to(0,0,0)\)이면 \(R\to0\)이므로 조임정리(Squeeze Theorem)에 의해 극한은 \(0\)이다.

답. \(0\).

채점 포인트 · 자주 하는 실수

  • (a) 서로 다른 값을 주는 두 경로와 각각의 극한값을 명시해야 한다. 한 경로의 값만 구하고 "존재한다"고 결론짓는 것은 틀리다.
  • (b) 경로 몇 개만 확인해서 \(0\)이라 하면 증명이 아니다. 부등식으로 위에서 누르고 조임정리를 쓴다.
  • \(|x|,|y|,|z|\le R\)를 이용한 상계 \(R^3\)의 근거를 쓴다.

(a)

Along \(y=0\), \((x,0)\to(0,0)\):

\[\frac{(\sin x)^2}{x^2}=\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\to1.\]

Along \(y=-x\), \((x,-x)\to(0,0)\), we have \(\sin y=-\sin x\), so

\[\frac{(\sin x-\sin x)^2}{2x^2}=0\to0.\]

The two paths give different limits, \(1\neq0\), so the limit does not exist.

Answer. The limit does not exist.

(b)

Factor the numerator: \(x^3yz+xy^3z-xyz^3=xyz\,(x^2+y^2-z^2)\). Let \(R=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\). Since \(|x|,|y|,|z|\le R\), we have \(|xyz|\le R^3\) and \(|x^2+y^2-z^2|\le x^2+y^2+z^2=R^2\). Hence

\[\left|\frac{xyz\,(x^2+y^2-z^2)}{x^2+y^2+z^2}\right|\le\frac{R^3\cdot R^2}{R^2}=R^3.\]

As \((x,y,z)\to(0,0,0)\), \(R\to0\), so by the Squeeze Theorem the limit is \(0\).

Answer. \(0\).

Key steps · Common mistakes

  • (a) State two paths that give different values, with each limit. Computing along one path only proves nothing.
  • (b) Checking a few paths is not a proof. Bound the expression above by something tending to \(0\) and apply the Squeeze Theorem.
  • Justify the bound \(R^3\) using \(|x|,|y|,|z|\le R\).

\(e^{y^2}\)은 \(y\)에 대해 초등함수로 적분되지 않으므로 순서를 바꾼다. 영역은 \(0\le y\le2,\ \dfrac y2\le x\le2y\)로 나타낼 수 있다(\(y\le2x\iff x\ge y/2\), \(y\ge x/2\iff x\le2y\)). 푸비니 정리(Fubini's Theorem)에 의해

\[\begin{aligned}\iint_D e^{y^2}\,dA&=\int_0^2\!\int_{y/2}^{2y}e^{y^2}\,dx\,dy\\&=\int_0^2\frac{3y}{2}\,e^{y^2}\,dy\\&=\frac34\Big[e^{y^2}\Big]_0^2=\frac34\,(e^4-1).\end{aligned}\]

답. \(\dfrac34\,(e^4-1)\).

(b)

각 적분의 \(x\)-범위와 \(y\)-범위를 읽는다.

  • 처음 두 적분: \(-1\le x\le2\), \(0\le y\le\frac23(x+1)\).
  • 나중 두 적분: \(2\le x\le6\)에서 \(0\le y\le\sqrt{6-x}\)와 \(\sqrt{6-x}\le y\le2\)는 \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\)에서 이어지므로 합치면 \(0\le y\le2\)이다. 곡선 \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\)는 영역 내부의 이음선일 뿐 경계가 아니다.

순서를 바꾸면 \(0\le y\le2\)이고, 왼쪽 경계 \(y=\frac23(x+1)\)을 \(x\)에 대해 풀면 \(x=\frac32y-1\), 오른쪽 경계는 \(x=6\)이다.

\[\int_0^2\!\int_{\frac32y-1}^{6}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.\]

  • (a) \(e^{y^2}\)은 \(dy\)로 먼저 적분할 수 없으므로 순서 바꾸기가 핵심이고, 영역을 그려 새 범위 \(0\le y\le2,\ y/2\le x\le2y\)를 근거와 함께 쓴다.
  • (b) 합쳐진 영역의 그림이 필수이다. \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\)를 경계로 착각하지 않는다(이음선).
  • 새 범위는 \(y\)의 상수 구간 \([0,2]\)과 \(x\)의 좌우 경계로 쓰고 \(dx\,dy\) 순서를 지킨다.

(a)

The first integral covers \(0\le x\le1,\ x/2\le y\le2x\) and the second covers \(1\le x\le4,\ x/2\le y\le2\). Together they form the triangle with vertices \((0,0),(1,2),(4,2)\) (figure).

Since \(e^{y^2}\) has no elementary antiderivative in \(y\), reverse the order. The region is \(0\le y\le2,\ \dfrac y2\le x\le2y\) (because \(y\le2x\iff x\ge y/2\) and \(y\ge x/2\iff x\le2y\)). By Fubini's Theorem,

\[\begin{aligned}\iint_D e^{y^2}\,dA&=\int_0^2\!\int_{y/2}^{2y}e^{y^2}\,dx\,dy\\&=\int_0^2\frac{3y}{2}\,e^{y^2}\,dy\\&=\frac34\Big[e^{y^2}\Big]_0^2=\frac34\,(e^4-1).\end{aligned}\]

Answer. \(\dfrac34\,(e^4-1)\).

(b)

Read off the ranges of the four integrals.

  • First two: \(-1\le x\le2\), \(0\le y\le\frac23(x+1)\).
  • Last two: for \(2\le x\le6\), the pieces \(0\le y\le\sqrt{6-x}\) and \(\sqrt{6-x}\le y\le2\) meet along \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\), so together they give \(0\le y\le2\). The curve \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\) is only an internal seam, not a boundary.

So the whole region is the trapezoid with vertices \((-1,0),(6,0),(6,2),(2,2)\) (figure). The line \(y=\frac23(x+1)\) passes through \((-1,0)\) and \((2,2)\).

Reversing the order: \(0\le y\le2\); the left boundary \(y=\frac23(x+1)\) gives \(x=\frac32y-1\), and the right boundary is \(x=6\).

\[\int_0^2\!\int_{\frac32y-1}^{6}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.\]

Answer. \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\!\int_{\frac32y-1}^{6}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy\).

Key steps · Common mistakes

  • (a) \(e^{y^2}\) cannot be integrated in \(y\) first, so reversing the order is the key; draw the region and justify the new limits \(0\le y\le2,\ y/2\le x\le2y\).
  • (b) A sketch of the merged region is required. Do not mistake \(y=\sqrt{6-x}\) for a boundary (it is a seam).
  • Write the new limits as a constant range \([0,2]\) for \(y\) and left/right curves for \(x\), with \(dx\,dy\) in that order.

(a)

The maximum rate of change of \(f\) is \(|\nabla f(P)|\), attained in the direction of \(\nabla f(P)\). So \(\nabla f(P)\) points along \(\langle2,-1,3\rangle\) and has length \(2\sqrt7\). Since \(|\langle2,-1,3\rangle|=\sqrt{14}\),

\[\begin{aligned}\nabla f(P)&=2\sqrt7\cdot\frac{\langle2,-1,3\rangle}{\sqrt{14}}=\sqrt2\,\langle2,-1,3\rangle\\&=\langle2\sqrt2,\,-\sqrt2,\,3\sqrt2\rangle.\end{aligned}\]

Answer. \(\nabla f(P)=\langle2\sqrt2,-\sqrt2,3\sqrt2\rangle\).

(b)

\(\nabla g(P)\) is normal to the level surface \(g=\sqrt2\) at \(P\). The tangent plane is parallel to \(x+2y-z=1\), so \(\nabla g(P)=k\langle1,2,-1\rangle\) for some constant \(k\). From (3),

\[D_{\mathbf u}g=\nabla g\cdot\mathbf u=\frac k3\,(1\cdot2+2\cdot(-1)+(-1)\cdot2)=-\frac{2k}{3}=4,\]

so \(k=-6\).

Answer. \(\nabla g(P)=\langle-6,-12,6\rangle\).

(c)

By the Product Rule, \(\nabla h=g\nabla f+f\nabla g\). Using \(g(P)=\sqrt2,\ f(P)=\tfrac12\) from (4):

\[\begin{aligned}\nabla h(P)&=\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2\,\langle2,-1,3\rangle+\tfrac12\langle-6,-12,6\rangle\\&=\langle4,-2,6\rangle+\langle-3,-6,3\rangle=\langle1,-8,9\rangle.\end{aligned}\]

The vector \(\langle-1,2,-2\rangle\) has length \(3\), so the unit vector is \(\mathbf v=\tfrac13\langle-1,2,-2\rangle\).

\[D_{\mathbf v}h(P)=\nabla h(P)\cdot\mathbf v=\frac{-1-16-18}{3}=-\frac{35}{3}.\]

Answer. \(-\dfrac{35}{3}\).

Key steps · Common mistakes

  • (a) State that the maximum rate is \(|\nabla f|\) and its direction is \(\nabla f/|\nabla f|\).

  • (c) Always use a unit vector (using \(\langle-1,2,-2\rangle\) directly is off by a factor of 3). Use \(\nabla h=g\nabla f+f\nabla g\) with the given values of \(f,g\).

Let the point of tangency be \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\). For the ellipsoid \(F=x^2+\frac{y^2}2+\frac{z^2}2=1\), \(\nabla F=\langle2x,\,y,\,z\rangle\). The first equation gives \(\lambda=\dfrac{4}{3+2m^2}\); substituting into the second gives \(\dfrac{16}{3+2m^2}=4\), so \(3+2m^2=4\) and \(m^2=\frac12\). Since \(m>0\), \(m=\frac{\sqrt2}2\). (Then \(\lambda=1\) and the point is \((\tfrac12,1,\tfrac{\sqrt2}2)\).)

Read off the \(xy\)-regions and the \(z\)-ranges. In both integrals \(z\) runs from the cone \(z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) to the sphere \(z=\sqrt{4-x^2-y^2}\) (\(\rho=2\)).

  • First: \(0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le x\): a triangle (\(0\le\theta\le\frac\pi4,\ r\le\frac1{\cos\theta}\)).
  • Second: \(1\le x\le\sqrt2,\ 0\le y\le\sqrt{2-x^2}\): inside the circle \(x^2+y^2=2\). For \(x\ge1\), \(\sqrt{2-x^2}\le x\), so \(y\le x\) holds automatically.

Together they form the sector \(0\le\theta\le\frac\pi4,\ 0\le r\le\sqrt2\) (the second region is the part beyond the side \(x=1\) of the triangle). Over this sector \(z\) lies between the cone \(z=r\) (\(\phi=\frac\pi4\)) and the sphere \(\rho=2\), which meet at \(r=\sqrt2,\ z=\sqrt2\). So in spherical coordinates the solid is

\[E=\{0\le\theta\le\tfrac\pi4,\ 0\le\phi\le\tfrac\pi4,\ 0\le\rho\le2\}.\]

The integrand is \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}=\rho\) and the volume element is \(\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi\), so

\[\begin{aligned}I&=\int_0^{\pi/4}\!\int_0^{\pi/4}\!\int_0^2\rho\cdot\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi\\&=\int_0^{\pi/4}\sin\phi\,d\phi\cdot\int_0^{\pi/4}d\theta\cdot\int_0^2\rho^3\,d\rho\\&=\Bigl(1-\frac{\sqrt2}2\Bigr)\cdot\frac\pi4\cdot4=\frac{\pi\,(2-\sqrt2)}{2}.\end{aligned}\]

Answer. \(\dfrac{\pi\,(2-\sqrt2)}{2}\).

Key steps · Common mistakes

  • Explain that the two Cartesian integrals make up one region (the sector \(0\le\theta\le\pi/4\), \(r\le\sqrt2\)); check that \(y\le x\) is automatic in the second piece.
  • The cone \(z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) is \(\phi=\pi/4\) and the sphere is \(\rho=2\); so \(0\le\phi\le\pi/4\).
  • Multiply the integrand \(\rho\) by the volume element \(\rho^2\sin\phi\) to get \(\rho^3\) (do not drop \(\sin\phi\)).
